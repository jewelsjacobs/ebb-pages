For rheumatoid arthritis

A symptom log your hands can manage on a flare day

RA tracking has a built-in irony: the days most worth recording — the flares, the mornings your hands won't cooperate — are the days a fiddly app is hardest to use. Long forms and joint-by-joint diagrams get abandoned exactly when they'd matter.

Ebb keeps it to one screen. You name what you want to track — joint pain, stiffness, fatigue, swelling — rate each one 1 to 5 with a few big taps, add a short note if you want ("right wrist and both knees" is plenty), and you're done. Thirty seconds, even mid-flare.

Why a daily record matters with RA

  • Rheumatology visits are short and far apart. Three months of days compress into one "how have you been?" — and memory favors this week over week four. A trend chart answers honestly, and Ebb exports to PDF so you can hand your rheumatologist a real record.
  • Medication changes take months to judge. Whether a new DMARD or biologic is actually helping is exactly the kind of slow, noisy change daily ratings make visible — including side effects, which you can track as their own line.
  • Flares have lead-ups. Many people find fatigue or stiffness creeping up before a full flare. A record you actually keep is how you spot your own pattern.

One honest note

Ebb is a diary, not a treatment. It doesn't diagnose, doesn't score disease activity, and is not a substitute for your rheumatologist or care team. It just makes sure that when someone asks how the last three months went, you have an answer that isn't a guess.
