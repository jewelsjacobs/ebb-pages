Appointment prep

How to bring a symptom log to your next appointment

You get fifteen minutes. Your doctor asks how you've been since last time, and three months of pain, fatigue, and bad nights collapse into "um… about the same, I think?" It isn't a memory failure. Recall is unreliable for anything that fluctuates, and chronic symptoms fluctuate by design.

A written record fixes that, but only if keeping it costs almost nothing. Here's a version that survives a flare.

Track few things, not everything

The instinct is to log every variable — food, weather, steps, sleep stages, mood, medication timing. That lasts about a week. Pick three to five symptoms that actually drive your appointments: the pain, the fatigue, the one that scares you. A short record you kept is worth more than a thorough one you abandoned in March.

Rate, don't describe

A 1–5 rating per symptom per day is enough to draw a line on a chart. Prose is harder to write on a bad day and harder for a clinician to skim. Keep one free-text note for the thing a number can't hold — "new rash," "couldn't stand up after the shower" — and leave it blank when nothing stands out.

Log daily-ish, not perfectly

Gaps are fine. A trend across ninety days survives a dozen missed entries; it does not survive you quitting because you broke a streak. Ebb has no streaks and no guilt mechanics on purpose.

Bring a document, not a phone

Handing over a phone and scrolling costs minutes you don't have. A one- or two-page summary — date range, symptoms, your daily ratings, your notes — can be read in twenty seconds and put in your chart. Ebb's premium tier generates that as a PDF of the last 30 days (see exactly what it looks like); free includes CSV export of your whole history if you'd rather build your own.

What a useful appointment report contains:

  • The date range it covers
  • Each symptom, with its average and its range
  • A chart showing direction — better, worse, or flat
  • The handful of notes that matter, not all of them
  • What changed: a new medication, a dose change, a new symptom

Say the three things first

Before you hand anything over, name what you want out of the visit: the symptom that's worst, the thing that's new, and the question you need answered. The log supports those three points — it doesn't replace them.

Common questions

What should I actually hand my doctor?

A short document, not a phone. Ebb's premium PDF covers the last 30 days — daily ratings and your notes in one table (here's a real sample) — so it can be printed or emailed ahead of the visit.

How far back should the report go?

Usually since your last appointment. You choose the date range; premium keeps unlimited history.

Do I need to log every single day for it to be useful?

No. Roughly-daily is enough to show a trend. Gaps don't invalidate the chart.
